# Japji Sahib — Pauri 2

## Gurmukhi (canonical)

ਹੁਕਮੀ ਹੋਵਨਿ ਆਕਾਰ ਹੁਕਮੁ ਨ ਕਹਿਆ ਜਾਈ ॥
ਹੁਕਮੀ ਹੋਵਨਿ ਜੀਅ ਹੁਕਮਿ ਮਿਲੈ ਵਡਿਆਈ ॥
ਹੁਕਮੀ ਉਤਮੁ ਨੀਚੁ ਹੁਕਮਿ ਲਿਖਿ ਦੁਖ ਸੁਖ ਪਾਈਅਹਿ ॥
ਇਕਨਾ ਹੁਕਮੀ ਬਖਸੀਸ ਇਕਿ ਹੁਕਮੀ ਸਦਾ ਭਵਾਈਅਹਿ ॥
ਹੁਕਮੈ ਅੰਦਰਿ ਸਭੁ ਕੋ ਬਾਹਰਿ ਹੁਕਮ ਨ ਕੋਇ ॥
ਨਾਨਕ ਹੁਕਮੈ ਜੇ ਬੁਝੈ ਤ ਹਉਮੈ ਕਹੈ ਨ ਕੋਇ ॥੨॥

## Scholars available for this pauri

Two scholars have translations on file for this pauri (no Sant Singh Khalsa translation was in the source for Pauri 2 specifically — don't imply otherwise).

### Bhai Manmohan Singh (English)

By the Lord's order bodies are produced. His order cannot be narrated. With His fiat the souls come into being and with His fiat greatness is obtained.

By His command the mortals are made high and low and by His written command they obtain woe and weal. Some obtain gifts through His order and some through His order are ever made to wander in transmigration.

All are subject to His fiat and none is exempt from His fiat. O Nanak! if man were to understand Lord's fiat, then no one would take pride (speak in ego).

### Prof. Sahib Singh (Punjabi teeka — word meanings + paraphrase)

**Word meanings:** ਹੁਕਮੀ = ਹੁਕਮ ਵਿਚ, ਅਕਾਲ ਪੁਰਖ ਦੇ ਹੁਕਮ ਅਨੁਸਾਰ। ਹੋਵਨਿ = ਹੁੰਦੇ ਹਨ, ਹੋਂਦ ਵਿਚ ਆਉਂਦੇ ਹਨ। ਆਕਾਰ = ਸਰੂਪ, ਸ਼ਕਲਾਂ, ਸਰੀਰ। ਜੀਅ = ਜੀਵ। ਵਡਿਆਈ = ਆਦਰ, ਸ਼ੋਭਾ। ਉਤਮੁ = ਸ੍ਰੇਸ਼ਟ, ਚੰਗਾ। ਬਖਸੀਸ = ਦਾਤ, ਬਖ਼ਸ਼ਸ਼। ਭਵਾਈਅਹਿ = ਜਨਮ ਮਰਨ ਦੇ ਗੇੜ ਵਿਚ ਪਾਏ ਜਾਂਦੇ ਹਨ। ਹਉਮੈ ਕਹੈ ਨ = ਹਉਮੈ ਦੇ ਬਚਨ ਨਹੀਂ ਆਖਦਾ, ਸੁਆਰਥੀ ਨਹੀਂ ਬਣਦਾ।

**Paraphrase:** ਅਕਾਲ ਪੁਰਖ ਦੇ ਹੁਕਮ ਅਨੁਸਾਰ ਸਾਰੇ ਸਰੀਰ ਬਣਦੇ ਹਨ, ਪਰ ਇਹ ਹੁਕਮ ਦੱਸਿਆ ਨਹੀਂ ਜਾ ਸਕਦਾ। ਰੱਬ ਦੇ ਹੁਕਮ ਅਨੁਸਾਰ ਹੀ ਸਾਰੇ ਜੀਵ ਜਨਮ ਪਾਂਦੇ ਹਨ ਅਤੇ ਹੁਕਮ ਅਨੁਸਾਰ ਹੀ ਸ਼ੋਭਾ ਮਿਲਦੀ ਹੈ। ਹਰੇਕ ਜੀਵ ਰੱਬ ਦੇ ਹੁਕਮ ਵਿਚ ਹੀ ਹੈ, ਕੋਈ ਜੀਵ ਹੁਕਮ ਤੋਂ ਬਾਹਰ ਨਹੀਂ ਹੋ ਸਕਦਾ। ਹੇ ਨਾਨਕ! ਜੇ ਕੋਈ ਮਨੁੱਖ ਅਕਾਲ ਪੁਰਖ ਦੇ ਹੁਕਮ ਨੂੰ ਸਮਝ ਲਏ ਤਾਂ ਫਿਰ ਉਹ ਸੁਆਰਥੀ ਜੀਵਨ ਛੱਡ ਦੇਂਦਾ ਹੈ।

*(Source: Translations — Bhai Manmohan Singh & Prof. Sahib Singh, via Srigranth.org)*

## Jarnail Singh — "Understanding Jap" (Sikhspectrum.com) — commentary on Hukam

This essay is long-form and treats Hukam (divine order/will) as the central concept of this pauri, engaging directly with the philosophical question of free will vs. determinism. Key points, attributed to Jarnail Singh's essay:

- Frames Pauri 2 as Guru Nanak's answer to a question that has "troubled mankind since day one": are we free agents, or is everything predetermined? Surveys three classical philosophical positions (Hard Determinism, Libertarianism, Soft Determinism / Compatibilism) before turning to what he argues is the Guru's own resolution.
- Argues Hukam shares God's own characteristics throughout Gurbani: it is **infinite** (cannot be fully described — cites p.53, Mahalla 1: "ਤੇਰਾ ਹੁਕਮੁ ਨ ਜਾਪੀ ਕੇਤੜਾ"), **irrevocable, fearless and impartial** (cites p.1047, Mahalla 3), **singular** (there is only one Hukam, just as God is one — cites p.948, Mahalla 3), **pre-existed the universe** (cites p.1035, Mahalla 1), and **pervades everywhere** (cites p.427, Mahalla 3; p.145, Mahalla 1).
- Argues Hukam is synonymous with Nam, Sabad, Guru, and Truth in Gurbani — not a narrow "instruction," but the operating principle of existence itself (cites p.72, Mahalla 1).
- On understanding Hukam: says obedience brings joy, disobedience brings suffering (cites p.400, Mahalla 5); that understanding Hukam is "the ultimate wisdom" surpassing all other knowledge (cites p.108, Mahalla 5); and — notably — that even Brahma, per Gurbani, failed to fully understand Hukam (cites p.423, Mahalla 3), which the essay uses to argue Sikhi does not equate Hindu deities with Akal Purakh.
- On accepting Hukam: argues true acceptance means total surrender of tan/man/dhan (body, mind, resources), that praise of God is itself an expression of accepting Hukam, that genuine acceptance is rare because ego (haumai) is Hukam's structural opposite, and — importantly — that acceptance requires God's own approval, not just a person's say-so (cites p.157, Mahalla 1; p.471, Mahalla 1).
- Frames "Manmukh" (mind-led) vs. "Gurmukh" (Guru-led) as the two categories Gurbani actually uses in place of concepts like "believer/atheist," arguing Sikhi has no real concept of an "atheist" in that sense — even someone who rejects religion is still, in this framing, acting under Hukam via their manmukh mind.
- Closes by tying the pauri's six lines back together: matter (Akar) takes shape by Hukam; the intangible (Jeea) likewise arises by Hukam; highness/lowness and pain/pleasure are apportioned by Hukam; no middleman can intervene in it; nothing exists outside it; and understanding it dissolves ego.

### Sant Teja Singh (English translation & commentary)

**Roman transliteration (Sant Teja Singh's own):**

Hukami howan akar hukam na kaiha jayee.
Hukami howan jee hukam milai wadyaee.
Hukami uttam neech hukam likh dukh sukh paiyeh.
Ekna hukami bakhsis ek hukami sada bhawaeeyeh.
Hukamai andar sabh ko bahar hukam na koey.
Nanak hukmai je bujhai ta howmai kahe na koey.

**Translation & commentary:**

Under the Divine law the whole universe is manifested, but this law is beyond the reach of words. It is inexpressible. Under that Law all the living creatures come into existence. Under that Law persons gain greatness. Under that Law some are born high and some low.

Under that Law people get pleasure and pain according to their Karmas. Under that Law some are blessed with the Divine grace, and get freedom from all desire, and the gift of self-realization. Under that Law some go on passing through endless cycles of birth and rebirth.

The whole Universe with its unlimited and boundless creation is controlled by this Divine Law. Sayeth Nanak, he who realizes this Divine Law and becomes at-one-with it, gets in tuned with the Universal Divine and loses all sense of egoism, mine and thine. He always submits himself to the divine will, and feels that the whole Creation is infused with the Divine spirit and all persons are brothers and sisters in spirit without any distinction of caste, creed, race or colour. Thus the veil of selfishness is removed and the Eternal Truth, the Divine within and without, is realized. Then one becomes God conscious.

*(Source: Teja Singh (Sant), MA, LLB (Punjab), AM (Harvard USA), *Japji Sahib*, The Kalgidhar Trust / Sant Attar Singh Hari Sadhu Ashram, Gurdwara Baru Sahib — Fourth Edition, March 2001. Text extracted from the user's copy of the PDF.)*
