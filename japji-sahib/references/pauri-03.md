# Japji Sahib — Pauri 3

## Gurmukhi (canonical)

ਗਾਵੈ ਕੋ ਤਾਣੁ ਹੋਵੈ ਕਿਸੈ ਤਾਣੁ ॥
ਗਾਵੈ ਕੋ ਦਾਤਿ ਜਾਣੈ ਨੀਸਾਣੁ ॥
ਗਾਵੈ ਕੋ ਗੁਣ ਵਡਿਆਈਆ ਚਾਰ ॥
ਗਾਵੈ ਕੋ ਵਿਦਿਆ ਵਿਖਮੁ ਵੀਚਾਰੁ ॥
ਗਾਵੈ ਕੋ ਸਾਜਿ ਕਰੇ ਤਨੁ ਖੇਹ ॥
ਗਾਵੈ ਕੋ ਜੀਅ ਲੈ ਫਿਰਿ ਦੇਹ ॥
ਗਾਵੈ ਕੋ ਜਾਪੈ ਦਿਸੈ ਦੂਰਿ ॥
ਗਾਵੈ ਕੋ ਵੇਖੈ ਹਾਦਰਾ ਹਦੂਰਿ ॥
ਕਥਨਾ ਕਥੀ ਨ ਆਵੈ ਤੋਟਿ ॥
ਕਥਿ ਕਥਿ ਕਥੀ ਕੋਟੀ ਕੋਟਿ ਕੋਟਿ ॥
ਦੇਦਾ ਦੇ ਲੈਦੇ ਥਕਿ ਪਾਹਿ ॥
ਜੁਗਾ ਜੁਗੰਤਰਿ ਖਾਹੀ ਖਾਹਿ ॥
ਹੁਕਮੀ ਹੁਕਮੁ ਚਲਾਏ ਰਾਹੁ ॥
ਨਾਨਕ ਵਿਗਸੈ ਵੇਪਰਵਾਹੁ ॥੩॥

### Jarnail Singh — "Understanding Jap" (Sikhspectrum.com) — commentary on Pauri 3

This essay reads Pauri 3's eight "Gaavai ko..." lines as a catalogue of competing folk/religious pictures of God — praising God's power, God's gifts (mistaking the gift for the Giver), God's virtues, God's unfathomability, God's power over life and death, God as far away, God as immediately present — which Guru Nanak lists in order to contrast with his own view in the pauri's final six lines. Key points, attributed to Jarnail Singh's essay:

- Argues Sikhism deliberately avoids "omnipotent/omniscient superpower" language for God, because that framing still implicitly assumes some opposition God has to overcome. Instead it uses "Karta" — the one who causes everything through Hukam, not a craftsman assembling parts (e.g. God doesn't manufacture a rose petal by petal, but causes the rose plant to bloom).
- On "praising the gift, not the Giver" (line 2): warns against devotion that fixates on the blessing rather than its source, citing SGGS p.755, p.676, and p.767.
- On "virtues" (line 3): argues singing God's virtues isn't the point — imitating them in one's own conduct is, quoting Kabir's "har jan aisa chahiye jaisa har hi hoye" (p.1372, "the servant of God should be a replica of God"). Worship in Sikhi, per the essay, is inculcating divine virtues in daily life, not ritual praise.
- On Agam/Agaad, "unfathomable" (line 4): argues Gurbani calls God unfathomable in totality but never unapproachable — citing God described as a close friend (sakha/mit, p.32) who meets people at their own level, unlike doctrines reserved for a specialist priestly class.
- On "far away" vs. "immediately present" (lines 7–8): reads Gurbani as rejecting both extremes in favor of *Bharpoor* (all-pervading fullness), citing p.25, p.515, and p.1340.
- Reads the closing six lines as Guru Nanak's own conclusion: countless attempts to theorize God all eventually give up ("Kath kath kathi koti kot kot" — millions have tried and tried but gave up at the end); the one stable fact is that God's Hukam has sustained and fed all of creation "since ages and ages," carried out in blissful, carefree ease ("Nanak wigsai wey parwah").

### Sant Teja Singh (English translation & commentary)

**Roman transliteration (Sant Teja Singh's own):**

Gawai ko tan howai kisey tan. Gawai ko daat janai nisan.
Gawai ko gun wadyaeean char. Gawai ko widya wikham vichar.
Gawai ko saaj karai tan kheh. Gawai ko jee lai phir deh.
Gawai ko japai dissai dur. Gawai ko waikhai hadra hadur.
Kathna kathi na away toat. Kath kath kathi koti kot kot.
Denda dey laindey thak payeh. Juga jugantar khahi khayeh.
Hukami hukam chalai raah. Nanak wigsai wey parwah.

**Translation & commentary:**

In this Pauri, Satguru Nanak Dev Ji shows that limited human power and intellect cannot fully express in spoken words His omnipotence, His unlimited gift, His beautiful qualities, and the depth of His wisdom.

Who has the power to express in words His omnipotence?

Who can appraise His gifts and attributes?

Who can sing the praise of His noble and beautiful qualities?

Who can express in words His vast knowledge, and the unfathomable reason (power of right thinking)?

Who can express His greatness, Who creates all life and then takes it back to the dust, from which it sprung?

Or who can find power to praise Him who takes away all and again brings it into existence?

Who can sing in words, Him who appears far removed from us?

Who can sing in ecstasy, Him who is omnipresent and reveals Himself all around in the creation (Nature)?

Since the creation of the world, millions of persons have sung the praises of the above-mentioned qualities, but their limit has not been reached in words one by one. He can be realized, but words have no power to express His unlimited qualities and power. He is the tireless bestower of unlimited gifts. The receivers are weighed down with His bounties. They enjoy them from age to age. They spend their span of life, and then pass away. This process of giving and taking goes on from age to age, cycle to cycle, sayeth Nanak.

The Divine master controls the immutable Divine Law, yet remains unattached, ever joyful and self-contained.

*(Source: Teja Singh (Sant), MA, LLB (Punjab), AM (Harvard USA), *Japji Sahib*, The Kalgidhar Trust / Sant Attar Singh Hari Sadhu Ashram, Gurdwara Baru Sahib — Fourth Edition, March 2001. Text extracted from the user's copy of the PDF.)*
